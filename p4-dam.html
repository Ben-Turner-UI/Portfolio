The problem

P4 DAM needed to keep delivering features after losing much of its front-end capacity.

I had been working in Figma and handing designs to developers. With fewer developers available, I needed to take more of that work through to implementation and reduce the time spent explaining how it should behave.

Role and team

I designed features and contributed production front-end code. The two front-end developers reviewed my changes and built the remaining work.

My product manager set priorities and tracked feature delivery. He also built an AI system that gathered feedback from the repository. I used that context in feature discussions and built the Claude tools that prepared our specifications and handovers.

Key decisions

Decision What I did Why
I clarified requirements before building I worked through a consistent set of questions with my product manager before starting each feature. I captured the answers in notes, then used them to prepare a specification. This gave me a clearer starting point and gave the developers’ AI tools the context they needed to help with implementation.
I worked in the product’s codebase I started building features in code, submitting changes on branches and working through developer reviews. This let me contribute to implementation while the developers checked my work against the product’s standards.
I turned repeated handover work into reusable tools I built Claude skills with instructions for generating specifications and handover documents. They also produced a snapshot file showing the interface in its different states. Developers could see the intended behaviour and use the files with their own AI tools, rather than relying on a planning meeting to fill in the details.

Design changes

One feature I released added realistic lighting to P4 DAM’s 3D asset viewer.

The viewer previously showed assets against a flat grey background. I added switchable image-based environments so reviewers could inspect a model under different lighting conditions and judge its shape and materials.

Impact

The team released 25% more features than six months earlier, according to my product manager’s tracking.

Planning meetings fell from around an hour to 30 minutes per feature because there were fewer handover questions to resolve.

One workflow and AI skill I created turned the transcript of a feature discussion into a structured specification. It won Best Designed at Perforce’s 2026 AI hackathon and is now used by other teams and designers across the business.

How I used AI

Claude helped prepare specifications and handovers. I also used it to help write front-end code, which I reviewed before submitting for developer review.

The output still needed correction. In one example, Claude generated Tailwind styles with values that did not match our design system. I replaced them with the classes used in the product.

What I would’ve done differently

I would have involved the developers earlier when building the specifications and handover tools.

I assumed what they would need in parts of the documentation. Later conversations revealed gaps that a discussion at the start could have caught.

Next time, I would review an early specification with the developers before using it as the template for other features.
