The problem

Budibase is an open-source low-code platform for internal apps like customer portals, admin panels and tools. Customers can self-host it and keep their data on their own infrastructure. As the in-house Product Designer I led the redesign of the automation builder.

The builder worked, but you could not see your data. People could not tell what data was flowing into or out of a step, or even what they had selected in the tab they were in. Debugging was guesswork, errors did not explain themselves, and complaints kept landing in the community Discord, eight to ten a month on automations alone.

Role and team

I designed and ran a paid Maze study with 25 active users and presented the findings to the CEO, CMO and Head of Engineering. I then designed the redesign and helped build it alongside the executive team and the lead developers.

Summary of user pain points

Themes grouped from the open-text answers on the hardest part of building an automation, feature requests and other comments.

Theme What users told us What they asked for Users
Testing and debugging Users could not easily check what went into a step or what came out. Test mode gave little information about what went wrong, step outputs were hard to decipher, and JavaScript steps could not be tested. Better error information, easier viewing of step outputs, a run tree, console output and a test sandbox. 10
Getting started and guidance Choosing a trigger and knowing what a field value meant was difficult. Some could not find a detailed guide or help with OAuth, or were unsure where to start. Hints in the builder, a written step-by-step guide, a beginner video, and more templates and example use cases. 8
Loops and branching limits Loops mixed up step results, and branching only supported one path at a time. This forced separate automations for create and update. Branching as its own step that works inside loops, plus more complex looping and conditions. 3
Limited building blocks Blocks were too limited for some jobs, leading to workarounds and external scripts. Even deleting a row needed extra queries and display-column values. A JavaScript editor with access to the platform’s environment. 2
Complex data and technical knowledge Wrapping and unwrapping JSON was hard, and users without a programming background found errors difficult to understand. Not specifically requested. 2

Themes are a manual grouping of the open-text responses. A user can appear in more than one theme, so counts do not add up to the number of respondents.

Design changes

Here are the design changes I made based on the user research above. They directly address the biggest concern: a lack of testing and debugging for the workflows.

Improvement Change Benefit
Data In / Data Out I added inline tabs to show the data flowing into and out of each step of a workflow. People can see what a step received and returned, and trace a wrong value back to where it started.
Inline Error Logging I added a dedicated tab with clear error messages for any failed step. A failed step says what broke, so people can fix it without asking on Discord.
New Sidebar Panel I moved workflow controls out of the main canvas and into a dedicated, contextual sidebar. The canvas shows the flow; the selected step’s settings live in one place.
Enhanced Canvas & UI I added zoom and pan, and made the layout more compact. Large workflows fit on screen and stay readable.

Impact

Before launch, automations drew eight to ten complaints and feedback requests a month on the Budibase Discord. After I shipped the redesign, that dropped to zero. Clearer errors meant fewer people needed help in the first place.

Automations are what Budibase called workflows before AI agents existed, and the redesigned builder became the foundation of its next product. Budibase’s agents feature is built directly on automations. An agent can take a new email, classify it and open a Jira ticket for the right team. When a step fails, the same structure shows what went in, what came out and what broke, then lets a person confirm the next action. Budibase now positions itself as an AI workflow toolkit.

How I used AI

I used ChatGPT on the Pro plan, the only AI tool we had available at the time. We did not have Claude Code, Codex or Cursor.

What I would’ve done differently

I would test the design after launch, to check customers were satisfied and to find further improvements. We assumed from the drop in complaints that we had done a good job. For a feature that ended up changing the direction of the company, I would have liked to have tested it but other design and research priorities came up.
