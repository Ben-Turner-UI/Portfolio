Making workflows easier to debug

The problem

Budibase’s workflow builder, called Automations, lets users connect a trigger to a series of actions. Users could build workflows, but struggled to see what each step received, returned or failed to process.

Recurring community feedback pointed to this problem. I ran a paid research study to understand it alongside other issues in the builder and establish where a redesign would have the most value.

Role and team

I owned the research and design. I planned and ran the Maze study, analysed the responses and presented the findings to the CEO, CMO and Head of Engineering.

I worked with the Head of Product to turn those findings into a direction for the builder, then developed and refined the interactions with developers through implementation.

Debugging stood out as the most disliked aspect. Users were more positive about the builder’s appearance and functionality. The findings pointed towards improving how users understood a workflow once it had been built.

What users needed help with

I grouped the open-text responses about difficult tasks, feature requests and other feedback into these themes.

Theme What users struggled with What they wanted Users mentioning the theme
Testing and debugging Seeing step inputs and outputs, understanding failures and testing JavaScript steps. Clearer errors, easier inspection of outputs, console output and better testing tools. 10
Getting started and guidance Choosing triggers, understanding field values and finding setup guidance. Hints, step-by-step guidance, videos, templates and examples. 8

Key decisions

Prioritise understanding and diagnosing failures.

The research raised requests for guidance, branching and additional building blocks. I prioritised debugging because it stood out in both the study and recurring community feedback.

I took that evidence to the executive team to make the case for improving access to step data and errors.

Keep debugging detail beside the workflow.

I reviewed approaches in products including Stack AI, n8n and Airtable. One option was to expand steps on the canvas to show their data and errors.

Large amounts of detail would take space away from the overall flow. I chose to show the selected step’s inputs, outputs and errors in the right sidebar. Users could inspect one step at a time while keeping the workflow visible.

Give each step a consistent structure.

I worked with the Head of Product on a reusable step structure that brought configuration, data and errors into a consistent experience. The same structure could support future workflow features.

Design changes

Improvement Change Benefit
Data In / Data Out Brought the selected step’s inputs and outputs into the right sidebar. Users can follow how a step handles data.
Error inspection Added an Errors view for the selected step. Users can inspect failure details beside the workflow.
Contextual sidebar Brought configuration and debugging information into the same area. Users can move between a step’s setup and its results in a consistent place.
Compact canvas Made the layout more compact and added zoom and pan. Users can navigate larger workflows with more of the flow in view.

How I used AI

I used ChatGPT to help organise the research findings and explore recommendations for the executive proposal. I checked its analysis against the responses and worked with the Head of Product to decide the final direction.

What I would have done differently

I would have tested the redesigned builder with users after launch.

The decline in negative feedback did not tell us how quickly people diagnosed errors or where they still struggled. A follow-up study would have helped answer those questions. Other startup priorities took over before we completed that work.
