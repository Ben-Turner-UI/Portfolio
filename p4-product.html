Fixing critical navigation and user flow bottlenecks in enterprise 3D libraries

Context

At Perforce, I am the product design lead for P4 DAM, a digital asset management platform used by creative and technical teams in games, film, and VFX. Customers include Electronic Arts, Pinewood Studios, Xbox Game Studios, and Meta.

P4 DAM sits on top of Perforce version control. Studios store enormous libraries of 3D models, textures, and media. They need to find the right file, understand where they are in the hierarchy, preview it quickly, and decide whether it is ready for review. If any step is slow or unclear, users abandon the product for specialist tools.

My job was to own the product direction for the day-to-day experience: what users see first, how they search and filter, how they navigate folders, and how they review assets.

Why this work existed

Sales. Competitors’ UX was getting more current, raising the expected standard. At the same time, observing users on calls, watching internal staff use the product, and reading engineer Slack channels revealed a consistent pattern: people were using the browser back button or bookmarks instead of the built-in navigation.

The original concept was an all-encompassing DAM that tried to do everything at once. I stripped that back: get people to the asset, and once they are there, let them manage it.

What I changed, and why

Each change outlines the problem, the decision, and the interface. Drag the sliders to see the before and after.

Search history

Users repeatedly searched for the same items, and the filters reset every time. Search history turns a repeat search into a single click instead of a rebuild.

Result: Repeat searches reduced to one click.

Advanced filtering

The old filtering lacked discoverability. When users applied a filter—especially dates—the UI failed to show what was active. You could change your view and have no way to verify what changed. I redesigned the system so active filters remain visible on the results and are easy to clear.

Result: Users can see and clear applied filters without guessing.

Breadcrumbs

The product lacked proper breadcrumbs. With no central home to anchor the trail and inconsistent placement between the sidebar and top bar, going back could land you somewhere different each time. People fell back on the browser’s back button to navigate deep asset hierarchies. I built one consistent pattern for the entire product.

Result: One universal breadcrumb pattern replaced reliance on the back button.

Previewing assets

Assets can exist in multiple projects, and users struggled to open them in the correct one. The new preview allows users to select their target project before opening the asset, showing them their destination before they commit.

Result: Opening an asset in the correct project is no longer a guess.

Dashboard

The old landing screen did not help anyone get to work. The redesign surfaces recent work and clear entry points up front, removing visual noise so people get to their assets faster.

Result: Fewer steps between opening the product and reaching the target asset.

Conclusion

As lead designer, I focused on structural friction rather than cosmetic polish. The shipped changes compound: users can find files faster, stay oriented, preview earlier, and navigate libraries with confidence. The result is a DAM experience that matches how game, film, and VFX teams actually work.
