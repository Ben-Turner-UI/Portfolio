Fixing critical navigation and user flow bottlenecks in enterprise 3D libraries

Context

At Perforce, I am the product design lead for P4 DAM, a digital asset management platform used by creative and technical teams in games, film, and VFX. Customers include Electronic Arts, Pinewood Studios, Xbox Game Studios, and Meta.

P4 DAM sits on top of Perforce version control. Studios store enormous libraries of 3D models, textures, and media. They need to find the right file, understand where they are in the hierarchy, preview it quickly, and decide whether it is ready for review. If any step is slow or unclear, users abandon the product for specialist tools.

My job was to own the product direction for the day-to-day experience: what users see first, how they search and filter, how they navigate folders, and how they review assets.

Why this work existed

Sales. Competitors’ UX was getting more current, raising the expected standard. At the same time, observing users on calls, watching internal staff use the product, and reading engineer Slack channels revealed a consistent pattern: people were using the browser back button or bookmarks instead of the built-in navigation.

The original concept was an all-encompassing DAM that tried to do everything at once. I stripped that back: get people to the asset, and once they are there, let them manage it.

What changed

Each row is the problem, the design change, and the outcome.

Search history Search had no memory. Users repeated the same searches, and filters reset every time. I added recent searches to the search experience. Repeat searches reduced to one click.
Advanced filtering Applied filters were hard to discover and easy to lose, especially date filters. I redesigned filtering so active filters stay visible on results and are easy to clear. Users can see and clear applied filters without guessing.
Breadcrumbs Navigation was inconsistent. Users relied on the browser back button in deep hierarchies. I built one breadcrumb pattern for the entire product. One universal breadcrumb pattern replaced reliance on the back button.
Previewing assets Assets in multiple projects were hard to open in the right place. The preview lets users pick the target project before opening the asset. Opening an asset in the correct project is no longer a guess.

Conclusion

As lead designer, I focused on structural friction rather than cosmetic polish. The shipped changes compound: users can find files faster, stay oriented, preview earlier, and navigate libraries with confidence. The result is a DAM experience that matches how game, film, and VFX teams actually work.
