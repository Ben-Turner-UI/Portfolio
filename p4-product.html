Fixing critical navigation and user flow bottlenecks in enterprise 3D libraries

Context

At Perforce, I am the product design lead for P4 DAM, a digital asset management platform used by creative and technical teams in games, film, and VFX. Customers include Electronic Arts, Pinewood Studios, Xbox Game Studios, and Meta.

P4 DAM sits on top of Perforce version control. Studios store enormous libraries of 3D models, textures, and media. They need to find the right file, understand where they are in the hierarchy, preview it quickly, and decide whether it is ready for review. If any step is slow or unclear, users abandon the product for specialist tools.

My job was to own the product direction for the day-to-day experience: what users see first, how they search and filter, how they navigate folders, and how they review assets.

Why this work existed

Sales. Competitors’ UX was getting more current, raising the expected standard. At the same time, observing users on calls, watching internal staff use the product, and reading engineer Slack channels revealed a consistent pattern: people were using the browser back button or bookmarks instead of the built-in navigation.

The original concept was an all-encompassing DAM that tried to do everything at once. I stripped that back: get people to the asset, and once they are there, let them manage it.

What changed

Search history

ProblemSearch had no memory, forcing users to re-type identical queries and re-apply filters whenever they navigated.
Design changeI added persistent recent searches and saved filter states directly to the search bar.
ResultRepeat searches dropped to a single click, saving users around 15 to 20 seconds per lookup.

Advanced filtering

ProblemActive filters were hidden and easy to lose track of, particularly date ranges and metadata tags.
Design changeI redesigned the filter UI so active selections stay visible above results with one-click removal.
ResultUsers can see, adjust, and clear active filters immediately without resetting their whole search.

Breadcrumbs

ProblemFolder structures were deep and inconsistent, leading people to rely on browser back buttons and bookmarks to stay oriented.
Design changeI built a single, responsive breadcrumb pattern across the entire application.
ResultReplaced reliance on browser navigation and stopped users getting lost in nested folder trees.

Previewing assets

ProblemAssets stored across multiple projects frequently opened in the wrong context, forcing artists to close the file and switch workspaces manually.
Design changeThe updated preview drawer lets users pick their target project before opening.
ResultRemoved wrong-project opens across shared asset libraries.

Dashboard

ProblemThe landing page was cluttered with static widgets that did not help users start their daily work.
Design changeI replaced the noise with recent files, active reviews, and direct search entry points.
ResultReduced the steps needed to reach an active file from four clicks down to one.

Impact

Fixing core navigation rather than making cosmetic updates made the library faster and more predictable. Artists can locate files quickly, stay oriented in deep folder trees, and move assets through review without leaving their workflow.
